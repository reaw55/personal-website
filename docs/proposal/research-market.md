# Market Landscape: Thailand Tourism and Lesser-Known Destinations

*Research date: 5 Nov 2026. Figures from news reports of official TAT / Ministry of Tourism and Sports (MOTS) data unless noted. "approx." = not verified against the primary source.*

## Summary

- Thailand's foreign arrivals **fell to about 33.0M in 2025** (-7% YoY) from about 35.5M in 2024 (approx.). TAT has **revised the 2026 target down** from 36.7M to about **33M arrivals** and about **2.7 trillion baht** total tourism revenue.
- Revenue is **highly concentrated**. In 2025 the top 5 provinces (Bangkok, Phuket, Chon Buri, Surat Thani, Chiang Mai) took **70.25%** of tourism revenue. The other **67 provinces shared 18.66%**.
- The government is actively pushing **secondary cities** ("55 secondary provinces"), for example through the 2024 and 2025 personal income tax deductions with a 1.5x multiplier for secondary-province travel. Uptake has been reported as lukewarm.
- Digital supply is fragmented. TAGTHAi is a government-backed super-aggregator, Local Alike curates community-based tourism (CBT), and global OTAs and activity marketplaces focus on the top destinations. No one owns **AI-assisted discovery and planning for secondary destinations, linked directly to small local businesses**.
- Demand-side tailwinds are AI trip-planning adoption (roughly doubled 2024→2025), long-stay visitors on the DTV visa (35k+ applicants in the first year), and interest in sustainable and community travel.

## 1. Tourism numbers 2024–2026

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 target |
|---|---|---|---|
| International arrivals | approx. 35.5M | ~32.9–33.0M (-7.2% YoY) | 36.7M (original); **~33M (revised Sep 2026)** |
| Revenue from foreign tourists | approx. 1.6–1.7 tn THB | ~1.53 tn THB | ~1.6 tn THB |
| Revenue from domestic tourism | approx. 1.0 tn THB | approx. 1.0–1.1 tn THB | ~1.1 tn THB |
| Total tourism revenue | approx. 2.6 tn THB | ~2.6–2.7 tn THB (one ex-TAT analysis: 2.86 tn incl. all provinces) | 2.78 tn (original); **~2.7 tn (revised)**; 3 tn "stretch" |
| Domestic trips (Thai residents) | approx. 200M+ person-trips | approx. 200M person-trips | n/a |

Notes: TAT's 2026 strategy has been branded "Thailand Tourism Next" / "Amazing 5 Economy", with mega-events and celebrity campaigns (for example the "Lisa effect"). Long-haul arrivals grew in 2025 while short-haul markets, mainly China, weakened.

## 2. Government programmes for secondary cities / community tourism

| Programme | Details |
|---|---|
| **Visit Secondary Cities tax deduction 2024** | 1 May–30 Nov 2024; personal income tax deduction for spending on tourism in **55 secondary provinces** |
| **"Quick Big Win" tourism tax measures 2025** | Cabinet approved 21 Oct 2025; ran 29 Oct–15 Dec 2025. Deduction of up to **20,000 THB** for domestic travel, with a **1.5x multiplier (cap 30,000 THB)** in 55 secondary provinces plus selected districts in 15 provinces. Expected about 140k users and about 420M THB of forgone tax. SCMP reported weak uptake. |
| Corporate seminar deduction | 2x deduction for training/seminars held in Thailand; 1.5x when held in secondary provinces |
| "Thai Tiew Thai" (Thais travel Thailand) | Domestic travel fairs and packages; PM and Cabinet visited Lamphun to promote secondary cities (approx.; the "Tiew Thai" name is used for campaigns and fairs. I found **no confirmed TAT app called "Tiew Thai"**). |
| **TAGTHAi** | Government-backed public–private "super aggregator" app run by Thai Digital Platform Social Enterprise, launched in 2019. TAT signed an MOU with it in Jan 2024. It sells bundles through TAGTHAi Pass, including local experiences. |
| Amazing Thailand campaigns | TAT's umbrella brand. The 2025–26 focus is on events, "Amazing Thailand Grand Tourism and Sports Year", and spreading visitors to "emerging" or secondary destinations (approx.) |

**Visitor/revenue concentration (2025, ex-TAT governor's analysis of provincial data):**

| Group | Share of tourism revenue |
|---|---|
| Top 5 provinces (Bangkok, Phuket, Chon Buri, Surat Thani, Chiang Mai) | 70.25% |
| Top 10 provinces | 81.34% |
| Remaining 67 provinces | 18.66% |
| Phuket alone | ~540–546 bn THB, more than the 50 lowest provinces combined |
| Foreign share of revenue | Phuket 92%; Bangkok 69%. Secondary provinces rely mostly on lower-spending domestic tourists. |

## 3. Competitors and alternatives

| Player | Type | Strength | Weakness vs. our concept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAGTHAi** | Govt-backed super-app / aggregator | Official backing (TAT, AOT, banks, hotels), passes, SME onboarding | Aggregator UX; limited traction and visibility reported (approx.); not discovery-led for hidden gems |
| **Local Alike** | Social enterprise, CBT tours | Deep community ties; about 100 communities in 46 provinces (older figure); 70% of revenue goes to the community | Small team and funding; curated tours rather than a self-service planner |
| **TakeMeTour** | Marketplace for local private guides (approx.; Bangkok-based, founded ~2015) | Local-guide supply, custom tours | Guide-centric; skews to major cities (approx.) |
| **Klook / GetYourGuide / Viator** | Global activity OTAs | Huge demand, payments, reviews | Commission-heavy; inventory concentrated in Bangkok, Phuket, Chiang Mai and Pattaya; hard for micro-businesses to list |
| **Agoda / Booking / Airbnb** | Accommodation OTAs | Dominant for stays | Homestays in rural areas are often unlisted or uncompetitive |
| **Google Maps / Travel, TripAdvisor** | Search and reviews | Default discovery tool | Thin data and reviews for rural spots; no curation or local-benefit lens |
| **ChatGPT / Gemini / AI planners (e.g., Mindtrip, Layla)** | Generative itinerary tools | Fast, personalised plans | Hallucination risk; weak ground truth for small venues; no booking with local SMEs |

**Context:** The Local Alike founder has said Thailand has about 200 CBT groups that "lack a reliable platform" to reach customers. The MOTS/DASTA register of CBT communities is much larger (approx.).

## 4. Trends

| Trend | Evidence |
|---|---|
| AI trip planning | Global Rescue: AI use for trip planning rose from 11% (Oct 2024) to 24% (Jul 2025); 22% in fall 2025, 30% among non-US travellers. Phocuswright: 56% of US travellers use AI in trip planning. ABTA (UK): 4% → 8%. **Trust is low**: only 7% "almost always" trust it, and under 20% would let AI build a full itinerary. |
| Long-stay / digital nomads | Destination Thailand Visa (DTV), launched Jul 2024: 5-year multi-entry visa, about 10,000 THB fee (varies by country), 180-day stays extendable. **35k+ applicants in its first year.** Since 1 Jan 2025 applications are e-visa only. |
| Sustainable / community tourism | Government policy (secondary cities, CBT, DASTA) and operators such as Local Alike target "meaningful travel". Global surveys show a stated preference for sustainable travel, though there is a stated-vs-actual gap (approx.). |
| Market mix shift | Chinese arrivals are weaker and long-haul is growing. Long-haul visitors stay longer and are more likely to explore beyond the main hubs (approx.). |
| Domestic softness | High household debt dampens domestic secondary-province travel despite tax incentives (SCMP). |

## Gap / opportunity

1. **Discovery gap**: about 67 provinces earn under 19% of revenue. Travellers lack trustworthy, curated, bookable information about them, and global platforms optimise for top destinations.
2. **Supply-side digitisation gap**: homestays, CBT groups, small restaurants and craft makers are often missing from OTAs (because of commissions, language and onboarding friction). A simple, LINE-friendly, Thai-language onboarding flow could unlock this inventory.
3. **AI trust gap**: travellers use AI planners but don't trust them. An AI planner grounded in **verified local data**, such as community-confirmed opening hours, prices and access, is a clear differentiator.
4. **Policy alignment**: the platform's purpose matches government goals (secondary provinces, the tax-deduction schemes, CBT). There is room for partnerships with TAT, DASTA, provincial offices and TAGTHAi, and for e-tax-receipt integration so purchases can count toward deductions.
5. **Long-stay segment**: DTV holders and slow travellers have the time and the motivation to explore secondary provinces.

## SWOT (new entrant)

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Clear niche (secondary destinations and local SMEs); impact story that aligns with policy; AI plus verified local data; can be lean and mobile-first | No brand or traffic; cold-start on both sides of the marketplace; costly to build verified local content; small and seasonal transaction values |
| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
| Government push and tax incentives for secondary cities; partnerships with TAT, DASTA, TAGTHAi and CBT networks; DTV / long-stay and long-haul growth; AI planning adoption roughly doubling; ESG/CSR corporate travel (seminar deductions) | Klook, Agoda and Google adding AI planners; TAGTHAi or a government app crowding out the space; falling arrivals in 2025–26; weak domestic spending; reliance on policy schemes that change yearly; data accuracy and liability |
