# Research: Lesser-Known Destinations in Northern and Northeastern (Isan) Thailand

*Desk research for the trip-planning platform proposal. Compiled 2026-11-05 under a short time limit. Figures marked "approx." are estimates or come from aggregator listings; confirm them with operators before using them in pricing or marketing.*

## Summary

- **Government programme.** Thailand promotes "secondary cities" (เมืองรอง, *mueang rong*), meaning provinces with fewer than about 4 million visitors a year. TAT's 2018 "Amazing Thailand Go Local" campaign named **55 second-tier provinces** and gave tax deductions for trips, seminars and MICE events held in them ([TAT News](https://www.tatnews.org/2018/01/tat-launches-go-local-project-spread-benefits-tourism-across-local-communities-nationwide/), [TTG Asia](https://www.ttgasia.com/2018/01/26/tourism-tax-rebates-a-push-for-thailands-secondary-provinces/)).
- **Recent tax measures.** On 4 June 2024 the Cabinet approved a personal income-tax deduction of up to **15,000 THB**. It covered spending with tour operators, hotels and homestays in secondary provinces from 1 May to 30 Nov 2024, and required an e-tax invoice or receipt ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/payroll/forvis-mazars-payroll-flash-news-june-2024), [Muang Thai](https://www.muangthai.co.th/en/article/lifestyle/tax-measures-to-boost-local-tourism)). A measure approved on 21 Oct 2025 reportedly allows **1.5x the amount spent** in secondary provinces, up to 30,000 THB ([Nishimura & Asahi](https://www.nishimura.com/en/knowledge/publications/20251105-116686)). Whether it applies in 2026 is unconfirmed; check with the Revenue Department.
- **Why this matters for the proposal.** Bookings that produce e-tax invoices (homestays, registered tour operators) can carry a tax benefit for Thai travellers. The platform could flag partners that are "e-tax eligible" and located in a secondary province.
- **The full 55-province list was not retrieved** in this research. Most of the North (excluding Chiang Mai) and most of Isan are generally described as secondary, but **each province below needs to be checked against the official list**.
- **Typical cost levels.** Community homestays cost about 350 THB per person per night, with meals extra. Two-day/one-night community packages cost about 1,650–2,000 THB per person. English-speaking guides cost about 1,500 THB per day. Craft workshops cost about 250–800 THB.

## Notes per destination

**1. Nan.** This is a slow-travel destination built on community-based tourism. DASTA lists **Baan Bo Suak Homestay** at 350 THB per person per night. Meals cost 100 THB each, snacks 50 THB and a khantoke dinner 200 THB per person, with activities such as a wrist-binding ceremony charged separately ([DASTA](https://smartdastaapp.dasta.or.th/place/1421?lang=en)). A **Phu Kha** two-day/one-night homestay package with meals and activities costs 2,000 THB per person for groups of 2–5 and 1,650 THB per person for 6 or more ([Readme.me](https://en.readme.me/p/57161)). Both are good partner models: per-person pricing with bundled cultural activities.

**2. Phrae.** Teak heritage and *mo hom* indigo textiles make it a natural fit for craft-shopping and workshop itineraries. Lodging is cheap, with a budget hotel listed at about USD 16 per night ([Expedia/Travelocity listings](https://www.travelocity.com/Lampang-Province-Guest-Houses.d6242014-aaGuestHouses.Travel-Guide-Accommodation)). No local guide rates were found.

**3. Lampang.** It is easy to reach by train and combines heritage (carriages, temples) with ceramics. Guesthouses are listed at about USD 13–44 per night ([Travelocity](https://www.travelocity.com/Lampang-Province-Guest-Houses.d6242014-aaGuestHouses.Travel-Guide-Accommodation)). Ceramic studios are obvious partners for workshops.

**4. Mae Hong Son.** Mountain-loop trips and ethnic-minority villages are the draw. One operator charges **1,500 THB per day for an English-speaking guide**, plus 800 THB per day for the guide's accommodation ([My Chiang Mai Tour](https://mychiangmaitour.com/?p=13800)). Homestays start around USD 15 per night ([Booking via Routard](https://hotel.routard.com/homestay/city/th/mae-hong-son.en-gb.html?aid=304142)). One cost guide puts daily spending on food, transport and sightseeing at USD 8–16 ([Champion Traveler](https://championtraveler.com/price/cost-of-a-trip-to-mae-hong-son-th)).

**5. Loei.** The cool, dry season (Dec–Feb) gives clear mountain views. Phu Kradueng closes during the rainy season ([Travel Dojo](https://www.traveldojo.com/thailand/loei/), [Sonasia](https://sonasia-holiday.com/thailand-travel/loei)). A direct Phetprasert Tour bus runs from Mo Chit to Phu Ruea twice daily, taking about 8 h 10 m for 440–650 THB ([Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Bangkok/Phu-Ruea)). Overnight VIP buses reach Chiang Khan in 7–11 h for about EUR 15–21 ([12Go](https://12go.asia/en/travel/bangkok/chiang-khan/?z=3128323)). The local bus from Loei to Chiang Khan costs about 38 THB ([Travelfish](https://www.travelfish.org/transport/thailand/northeast_thailand/loei/chiang_khan/all)).

**6. Bueng Kan.** It suits nature and pilgrimage trips. The Living Community Museum opened in 2018 and can be combined with Phu Tok and Wat Ahong in a single day. Its entry fee is 50 THB according to one listing and free according to another ([Thai Holiday Guide](https://www.thaiholidayguide.com/attraction/bueng-kan-living-community-museum/)). **Bunloed's Huts** is a village homestay near Phu Wua and Wat Phu Tok ([Lonely Planet](https://www-lonelyplanet-com-57e2.imagizer.com/pois/1245088)). Guesthouses near Bung Khong Long offer activities such as alms-giving ([Tripniceday](https://www.tripniceday.com/en/place/เรือนไทยเกสต์เฮาส์)).

**7. Nakhon Phanom.** **Ban Na Thon**, the Tai Kuan community in That Phanom district, is promoted as a low-carbon cultural tourism route ([thailand.go.th](https://thailand.go.th/event-detail/001_02_075)). Operators run half-day and full-day handicraft visits there ([Bamboo Travel](https://bambootravel.co.uk/holidays-to-thailand/experience/traditional-handicrafts-at-nathon-cultural-village)).

**8. Sakon Nakhon.** It is the strongest craft-tourism story in Isan. **Kram Sakon** runs DIY indigo dyeing for 250–800 THB depending on the cloth, open daily ([Thailandee](https://www.thailandee.com/en/visit-thailand/kram-sakon-indigo-dyeing-sakon-nakhon-502)). Day trips to **Ban Nong San** include food, transport and tickets ([TakeMeTour](https://bangkokair.takemetour.com/trip/local-table-sakon-nakhon-ban-nongsan-indigo)). The province also holds an Indigo City Festival ([Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/sakon-nakhon-hosts-indigo-city-festival-at-the-king-rama-v-monument-plaza-march-28-30-495491)). TAT's BCG / Happy Model community-route e-book lists Ban Nong San ([TAT e-book](https://tourismproduct.tourismthailand.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/E-Book-%E0%B8%89%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%9A%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%95%E0%B9%87%E0%B8%A1-EN.pdf)).

**9. Buriram.** It offers Khmer heritage plus sport events (football, MotoGP circuit), which bring periodic demand spikes. Seasons and access times in the table are general knowledge and were not verified in this research.

**10. Surin.** Elephant culture (Kui community at Ban Ta Klang) and silk are the draws. Details are general knowledge and were not verified in this research. Partners should follow ethical-elephant standards.
