# Research: Pricing Benchmarks for a Thai Trip-Planning and Local-Guide Platform

_Compiled November 2026. Sources are mainly operator listings, platform supplier pages and trade press from 2020 to 2026. Figures marked **approx.** come from secondary sources, are old, or conflict between sources. Check them before using them in final financials._

## Summary

- **Guides:** A licensed Thai private guide charges approx. **1,000–1,500 THB per half day** and approx. **1,500–3,000 THB per full day** for the guide fee only, based on forum and guide quotes. Guide-plus-vehicle packages from operators run approx. **4,000–8,000 THB per day** (about US$110–230). A licensed English-speaking guide can be booked from approx. **US$132 per day**.
- **Licensing:** Guiding is a job reserved for Thai nationals. Every guide needs a licence from the Department of Tourism under the Tourism Business and Guide Act B.E. 2551 (2008). Foreigners cannot get work permits for the role and are arrested regularly when they guide anyway. **Verified across several news and legal sources.**
- **Marketplaces:** OTAs take **20–30%** (GetYourGuide confirms 20–30% on its own supplier site). Guide-first marketplaces take **20–32%** (Withlocals publishes 32%). Airbnb Experiences charges approx. 20%.
- **Planner apps:** Consumer trip-planner subscriptions cluster at **about US$40–50 per year** (Wanderlog Pro approx. $40, TripIt Pro approx. $49, Layla approx. $49). Most users stay on the free tier, so subscriptions alone are a weak revenue base.
- **Community-based tourism (CBT):** Village homestays cost approx. **900 THB per person per night including two meals**. Packaged multi-day CBT trips sold to foreigners cost approx. **US$100–200 per person per day** once guiding and transport are included.

**Language premium:** No published standard was found. Operators charge noticeably more for guides who speak less common languages (e.g., French, German, Russian, Japanese, Chinese) than for English speakers. **approx. +20–50%, unverified.** Ask local guide associations for quotes.

**Licensing requirements (verified):**
- The Tourism Business and Guide Act B.E. 2551 (2008) requires every tour guide to hold a licence from the Department of Tourism. Legal commentary says guides **must be Thai nationals**.
- Guiding is also a job reserved for Thai nationals under foreign-business and alien-work rules. Foreigners cannot get work permits for it. A former prime minister cited the Foreign Business Act of 1999 on this point in 2017.
- Penalties: a 2025 Department of Tourism statement gives up to 1 year in prison and/or a fine of up to **100,000 THB** for guiding without a licence. Another analysis cites up to 500,000 THB, so sources conflict. Foreign offenders are also charged under the Immigration Act. Recent arrests include Russian and Chinese nationals in Pattaya.
- Licences come in different badge types depending on scope (general or regional, foreign-language or Thai-language, specialist such as nature or culture). **approx., details not verified in this pass.**
- **Implication:** the platform's guides on the ground must be licensed Thai nationals. Foreign staff can work only in non-guiding roles such as product, content and support.

## Implications for our pricing

- **Guide supply cost:** budget about **2,000–3,000 THB per guide-day** for licensed guides in secondary provinces, and more in Bangkok, Phuket and Chiang Mai or for language specialists. Listings should show guide-only and guide-plus-vehicle prices separately.
- **Take rate:** a **15–20% commission** undercuts GetYourGuide, Viator, Withlocals and ToursByLocals (20–32%). That is a strong pitch to guides and community operators. A rate around 15% for community and CBT partners would support the "local benefit" story.
- **Price points to target:** private half-day experiences at approx. **US$40–90 per group**, full days at approx. **US$90–180 per group**, and CBT homestay add-ons at approx. **900–1,500 THB per person per night**. These sit below international marketplace prices while still paying guides above the forum-quoted floor.
- **Compliance as a differentiator:** check every guide's Department of Tourism licence before listing them, and show a "Licensed Thai guide" badge. Unlicensed guiding is criminal, and travellers worry about it. Our side of the business may need a tour-operator (business) licence. **Confirm with counsel.**
- **App monetisation:** keep AI trip planning **free**, or offer an optional premium tier at about **US$3–5 per month (US$25–40 per year)**, below Wanderlog, TripIt and Layla. Most revenue should come from booking commissions and affiliate links, not subscriptions.
- **B2B and partner revenue:** consider promoted listings and analytics subscriptions for local businesses (e.g., cafés and craft shops in lesser-known provinces). This mirrors Viator's paid-placement model.
- **Partnerships:** Local Alike and TakeMeTour are potential supply partners rather than only competitors. They already aggregate licensed guides and CBT communities.
